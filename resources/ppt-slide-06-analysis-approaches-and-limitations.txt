Slide 6: Possible Analyses & Their Limitations - Why MCA

Approach -> What it shows -> Limitation

1. Simple ranking / Pareto (one variable)
   - Shows: which single factor (machine, reason code) drives the most
     interruption time.
   - Limitation: can't see interactions between factors - misses
     compound causes entirely.

2. Manual cross-tab / pivot table (two variables, ad hoc)
   - Shows: basic co-occurrence between two chosen factors.
   - Limitation: manual and doesn't scale to many variable pairs; no
     significance test, so an apparent pattern could just be noise;
     someone has to guess which pair to check.

3. Correspondence Analysis - CA (exactly two variables)
   - Shows: statistically tested, visualized association between one
     pair of factors, with a significance test built in.
   - Limitation: still one pair at a time - can't capture 3-way (or
     more) compound effects.

4. Multiple Correspondence Analysis - MCA (three or more variables)
   - Shows: tested, visualized association across many factors at
     once - surfaces compound, hidden root causes at scale, with
     statistical confidence built in.
   - This is the only approach here that scales to real interruption
     complexity (many factors, many combinations) while staying
     statistically grounded - exactly what "capture all possible
     patterns" requires.

Used responsibly - limitations of MCA itself:
- Needs enough occurrences per combination to be reliable (handled in
  our tool with a minimum-count filter on lift/association tables).
- More factors and categories make the output map denser - our
  dashboard's per-variable and per-pair views keep it readable.
- Confirms statistical association, not mechanical causation - findings
  still need engineering judgment to confirm and act on.
